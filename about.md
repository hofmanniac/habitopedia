# About Habitopedia

Habitopedia is a comprehensive catalog of habits and practices sourced from books, podcasts, research papers, videos, articles, and expert advice. It's designed to help you discover, understand, and implement evidence-based habits across all areas of life.

## The Problem

We're bombarded with advice from books, podcasts, YouTube videos, articles, and social media. The same wisdom gets repeated across different sources, but:

- **It's scattered**: You heard something valuable in a podcast, but where was it again?
- **It's redundant**: The same advice appears across 20 different sources
- **It's unstructured**: No easy way to compare, filter, or discover related practices
- **It's overwhelming**: Too many options, not enough guidance on what works

## The Solution

Habitopedia organizes this wisdom into a searchable, cross-referenced database where you can:

✅ **Discover** habits by category, source type, time investment, or evidence level  
✅ **Understand** each habit with implementation guides, tips, and common pitfalls  
✅ **Track sources** to see which books/podcasts discuss which habits  
✅ **Find connections** through related habits, prerequisites, and habit stacks  
✅ **Filter intelligently** by difficulty, effectiveness, and time required  

## Features

### Rich Metadata
Every habit includes:
- **Evidence level**: Anecdotal → Expert Opinion → Research-Backed → Meta-Analysis
- **Time investment**: < 5 min to 1+ hour
- **Difficulty rating**: 1-5 scale
- **Effectiveness rating**: 1-5 scale based on impact
- **Category codes**: For easy organization (PROD-001, HEALTH-015, etc.)

### Multiple Discovery Paths
- Browse by **category** (Productivity, Health, Finance, Mindset, etc.)
- Browse by **source type** (Books, Podcasts, Videos, Research Papers)
- **Advanced search** with multiple filters
- **Statistics dashboard** showing trends and top sources

### Cross-Referencing
- **Related habits**: Discover similar or complementary practices
- **Prerequisites**: Habits to build first
- **Habit stacks**: Combinations that work well together
- **Source tracking**: See all habits from a specific book or podcast

### Evidence-Based
Each habit links to original sources with:
- Author/creator information
- Publication details
- Specific timestamps (for podcasts/videos)
- Notes on key insights

## Categories

Habits are organized into 10 main categories:

- **⚡ Productivity**: Focus, time management, and output
- **💪 Health & Wellness**: Physical and mental health
- **❤️ Relationships**: Building connections
- **💰 Finance**: Money management
- **🧠 Mindset**: Mental frameworks and resilience
- **📚 Learning**: Knowledge and skill acquisition
- **🎨 Creativity**: Innovation and expression
- **💬 Communication**: Speaking and listening
- **🏡 Environment**: Physical space optimization
- **🕉️ Spirituality**: Meaning and purpose

## How to Use This Site

### For Beginners
Start with the [Browse by Category](/habitopedia/categories/) page. Pick your priority area and explore habits rated "High Impact, Low Difficulty" (check the [Statistics](/habitopedia/stats/) page for recommendations).

### For Researchers
Use the [Advanced Search](/habitopedia/browse/) to filter by evidence level, ring, or source type to see all habits from your favorite books or experts.

### For Habit Builders
Look for **prerequisites** and **habit stacks** on individual habit pages. Build foundational habits first, then layer complementary practices.

## Philosophy

**Quality over Quantity**: We prioritize well-documented habits with clear sources over exhaustive lists.

**Evidence-Informed**: Every habit indicates its evidence level, from anecdotal stories to meta-analyses.

**Practical Implementation**: Focus on "how to start" rather than just theory.

**Cross-Referencing**: Habits don't exist in isolation — we map relationships and dependencies.

**Source Attribution**: Always credit original authors and experts who shared the wisdom.
