description: "Schedule specific blocks of time for different activities and tasks, treating them as non-negotiable appointments with yourself."
ring: 1
time_investment: short
how_to_start: |
  Start by blocking just tomorrow. Open your calendar and create blocks for your most important tasks. Be realistic: block 90 minutes for deep work, 30 minutes for email, etc. Include breaks, meals, and transition time. Don't over-schedule — aim for 60-70% of your day blocked, leaving buffer time.
tips:
  - "Block your most important work during your peak energy hours"
  - "Include buffer time between blocks for transitions and overruns"
  - "Color-code different types of blocks (deep work, meetings, admin, personal)"
  - "Schedule breaks and meals — don't skip them"
  - "Review and adjust your blocks at the end of each day for tomorrow"
  - "Protect deep work blocks fiercely — treat them like doctor appointments"
  - "Use themes for different days (e.g., Monday = strategic work, Tuesday = meetings)"
  - "Block personal time and self-care, not just work"
common_pitfalls:
  - "Over-scheduling every minute — this creates stress and rigidity"
  - "Not including buffer time between blocks"
  - "Abandoning the system after one 'bad' day — adjust and continue"
  - "Only blocking work, forgetting meals, breaks, exercise, family time"
  - "Making blocks too long without breaks (diminishing returns after ~90 min)"
  - "Not saying no to requests that conflict with your blocks"
  - "Treating the schedule as optional — commit to it like appointments"
success_metrics:
  - "Completing high-priority tasks consistently"
  - "Reduced decision fatigue about 'what to do next'"
  - "Better work-life balance (personal time is also blocked)"
  - "Less time spent on low-value activities"
  - "Reduced stress from feeling 'behind'"
  - "Improved focus during work blocks"
  - "Actually finishing at your intended end time"
