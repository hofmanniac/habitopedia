Your attention is your most valuable resource. Every notification is someone else deciding what you think about right now. Eliminating notifications is reclaiming that sovereignty.

## The Cost of Interruption

Research shows it takes an average of 23 minutes to regain focus after an interruption. If you're getting 10 notifications per day, that's nearly 4 hours of fragmented attention—every single day.

But the damage isn't just lost time. Each interruption:
- Fragments your thinking and prevents deep work
- Spikes stress hormones (your brain treats each ping as urgent)
- Creates cognitive residue (part of your mind stays on the previous task)
- Trains your brain to expect distraction (destroying your focus capacity)

## The Illusion of Necessity

"But what if it's important?"

Here's the reality: Almost nothing requires an instant response. Email can wait 2 hours. Slack messages can wait 30 minutes. Social media never requires an immediate response.

For two decades before smartphones, people survived just fine checking messages a few times per day. The expectation of instant availability is recent, artificial, and harmful.

## The Simple Fix

Turn them all off. Not most. All. Then add back only what's genuinely essential (usually just calls/texts from specific people).

You'll be shocked how little you miss.
