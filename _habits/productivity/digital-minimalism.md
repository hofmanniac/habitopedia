Digital minimalism isn't about rejecting technology—it's about using it intentionally rather than letting it use you.

Most people's relationship with technology is reactive and accidental: apps get downloaded, accounts get created, and before you know it, you're spending 3+ hours daily scrolling through feeds you don't even enjoy.

Digital minimalism reverses this. You start from zero and deliberately choose which tools serve your goals and values.

## The Attention Economy

Tech companies employ thousands of engineers to maximize your engagement (a polite word for addiction). Every notification, auto-play, infinite scroll, and recommendation algorithm is designed to keep you scrolling.

You cannot fight this with willpower alone. You need structural changes: removing apps, blocking sites, creating friction between impulse and action.

## Less but Better

Digital minimalism follows the same principle as physical minimalism: own fewer things of higher quality, used more intentionally.

Instead of:
- 7 social media accounts you passively scroll
- 50 apps on your phone you rarely use
- 100 email subscriptions you never read

You have:
- 1-2 platforms you actively engage with
- 10-15 essential apps
- 5-10 newsletters you actually read

The result isn't deprivation—it's liberation. You're not missing out; you're opting out of distraction to opt in to what matters.
