Your calendar is your operating system for time. Without it, you're navigating by memory, which inevitably leads to double-bookings, forgotten commitments, and reactive scheduling.

## Why It Matters

People who maintain calendars aren't more organized because they're naturally disciplined—they use a tool that makes organization easy. The calendar does the remembering so your brain doesn't have to.

Benefits of comprehensive calendar usage:
- **No more surprises**: You always know what's coming
- **Better decisions**: You can see opportunity costs of new commitments
- **Protected focus time**: Block it or lose it to meetings
- **Reduced anxiety**: Your brain trusts the system instead of worrying about what you're forgetting

## The Everything Rule

The key is putting EVERYTHING on your calendar, not just meetings:
- Appointments and meetings (obviously)
- Time blocks for important projects
- Recurring routines (exercise, family dinner, etc.)
- Personal commitments (kids' events, haircuts, etc.)
- Prep time before meetings
- Buffer/transition time

If it needs to happen, it goes on the calendar. If it's not on the calendar, it probably won't happen.
