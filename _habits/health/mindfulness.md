Most of life happens on autopilot. You shower while planning the day, eat while scrolling, drive while lost in thought. You're physically present but mentally elsewhere—living in memory or anticipation rather than reality.

Mindfulness is the practice of bringing attention back to now. Not as a peak experience or special state, but as a skill you can practice in any moment.

## The Default Mode

Your brain's default mode is wandering: replaying conversations, planning responses, worrying about outcomes, judging experiences. This serves an evolutionary purpose (predicting and learning from patterns), but it also means you miss most of your actual life.

Research shows the average person spends 47% of waking hours mind-wandering. Nearly half of life is spent not where you actually are.

Mindfulness doesn't eliminate mind-wandering (impossible). It builds awareness of when it's happening and the ability to return attention to present moment.

## Not Just Meditation

Mindfulness and meditation are related but different:
- **Meditation**: Formal practice of training attention (sitting still, focusing on breath, etc.)
- **Mindfulness**: Bringing that attention to everyday activities

You can meditate without being mindful during your day. The real power is applying mindfulness throughout normal life: eating, walking, listening, working.

## Why It Matters

Benefits of regular mindfulness practice:
- **Reduced anxiety**: Less time worrying about future
- **Less rumination**: Less replaying of past
- **Better focus**: Trained attention transfers to work
- **Improved relationships**: Actually listening instead of planning what to say
- **More enjoyment**: Experiencing life instead of thinking about it
- **Emotional regulation**: Noticing feelings before they control you

## The Practice

Start small: one activity per day done mindfully. Washing dishes. Brushing teeth. Walking to your car. During that activity, just notice what's happening. Physical sensations. Sounds. Temperature.

When your mind wanders (it will—constantly), that's not failure. Noticing it wandered means you just became aware. Gently return attention to the present. That return is the practice.
