Modern life doesn't have a natural wind-down. We go from work meetings to emails to bright screens to bed, expecting our brains to instantly shut off. It doesn't work that way. You need a transition period—a deliberate off-ramp from the day's demands.

## The Transition Problem

Your body and mind need time to shift gears. Going from high-intensity work mode to sleep mode isn't like flipping a switch; it's like landing a plane. You need a descent path.

Without an evening wind-down:
- Your mind races when you lie down (replaying the day, planning tomorrow)
- Stress hormones remain elevated when melatonin should be rising
- Screen exposure suppresses melatonin production
- You're tired but wired—exhausted yet unable to sleep

With a wind-down routine:
- You signal to your body that it's time to rest
- Stress hormones naturally decline
- Melatonin production begins on schedule
- Your mind empties its cache of worries and to-dos
- You arrive at bed calm and ready for sleep

## The Science of Wind-Down

Your body runs on a circadian rhythm that responds to environmental cues: light, temperature, routine. An evening wind-down leverages these:

**Light**: Dimming lights 1-2 hours before bed triggers melatonin production
**Temperature**: Cooling down (cool room + warm shower paradoxically helps)
**Routine**: Consistent cues tell your body "sleep is coming soon"
**Mental offload**: Capturing tomorrow's tasks prevents rumination

This isn't woo-woo; it's working with your biology instead of against it.
