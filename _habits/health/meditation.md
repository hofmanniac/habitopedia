Meditation isn't about stopping thoughts or achieving some blissed-out state. It's about training your attention—noticing when your mind wanders and gently bringing it back. This simple practice has profound effects.

## The Attention Gym

Think of meditation as strength training for your mind. Each time your attention wanders and you bring it back, you're doing a mental rep. Over time, this builds your capacity to focus, regulate emotions, and respond rather than react.

Research shows consistent meditation practice:
- Reduces anxiety and depression
- Improves focus and working memory
- Increases emotional regulation
- Reduces stress hormone levels
- Literally changes brain structure (more gray matter in attention/emotion regulation areas)

## The Paradox

The hardest part of meditation is also proof it's working: your mind will wander constantly. Beginners think this means they're bad at meditation. Actually, noticing that your mind wandered means you just became aware—that's the practice.

The goal isn't a perfectly quiet mind. The goal is awareness of when your mind isn't quiet and the ability to redirect it. This skill transfers to everything: noticing when you're scrolling mindlessly, reacting emotionally, or getting distracted from priorities.

## Start Absurdly Small

Most people start with 20-minute sessions, hate it, and quit. Start with 2 minutes. Yes, 2 minutes. After a week, go to 5. After another week, maybe 10. The consistency matters far more than the duration.
