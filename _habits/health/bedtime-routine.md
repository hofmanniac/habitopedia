Sleep doesn't start when your head hits the pillow. It starts with the signals you send your body in the hour before bed. A bedtime routine is that signaling system.

## Classical Conditioning for Sleep

Remember Pavlov's dogs? Consistent cues led to conditioned responses. A bedtime routine works the same way: the same sequence of actions every night conditions your body to expect sleep.

After a few weeks of consistency:
- Your body starts releasing melatonin at routine time
- Your brain recognizes the cues and begins shutting down
- Falling asleep becomes easier and faster
- Sleep quality improves

Without a routine, you're sending mixed signals: sometimes screens right before bed, sometimes reading, sometimes work, sometimes exercise. Your body never knows what's coming.

## The Power of Consistency

The specific activities in your routine matter less than doing them consistently. Whether you shower, journal, stretch, or read—what matters is the same sequence, same time, every night.

Your routine becomes a bridge from wakefulness to sleep. Each step takes you closer to rest mode.

## Non-Negotiables

Regardless of your specific routine, include these elements:
- **Fixed start time**: Anchor your routine to the clock, not tiredness
- **No screens**: Blue light suppresses melatonin
- **Cool environment**: Slight temperature drop triggers sleep
- **Low stimulation**: Calm activities, not problem-solving or exciting content
- **Phone away**: Either another room or do not disturb mode

The goal is creating optimal conditions for your body to do what it already knows how to do: sleep.
