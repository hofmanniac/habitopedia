Daily reading is one of the highest-leverage habits you can build. Warren Buffett reads 500 pages per day. Bill Gates reads 50 books per year. Not because they have more time than you—they make time because they understand the compounding returns on knowledge.

## The Compounding Effect

If you read just 15 minutes per day (about 20 pages), you'll read:
- ~20-25 books per year
- 200+ books per decade
- 1,000+ books over a lifetime

That's not just information consumption. That's access to thousands of hours of thinking from the smartest people who've ever lived. It's learning from other people's mistakes, studying systems that work, and developing mental models that compound in value.

## Beyond Entertainment

There's nothing wrong with reading for pleasure, but daily reading as a habit is about something more: **deliberate learning**. You're actively choosing to spend time with ideas that challenge you, expand your thinking, and give you leverage in life and work.

Each book is a conversation with someone who spent years developing expertise. For $15 and a few hours of your time, you get direct access to their best thinking. There's no better ROI.

## The Attention Muscle

Daily reading also builds your attention span in an age of distraction. Sitting with a single idea for 30 uninterrupted minutes becomes increasingly rare—and valuable. The ability to focus deeply on complex material is a competitive advantage.
